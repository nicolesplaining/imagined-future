# Publication assessment and claim boundary

## Bottom line

The evidence now supports a conference paper whose central claim is that
coherent imagined futures directionally control action in two generations of
world action models. Cosmos Policy establishes early-state steering across all
ten LIBERO-10 tasks and strong state dependence. Cosmos 3 supplies a frozen
population replication: 22 independent clusters across six RoboLab tasks show
near-complete donor steering in action, corresponding physical execution, and
population-level mediation through future-token K/V content. The task-balanced
analyses agree with the state-weighted estimates.

The content claim must be model-specific. In Cosmos Policy, wrist/primary
camera interventions and rare natural matches favor prospective visible robot
motion over isolated object-state consequences. In Cosmos 3, a 10-cluster,
six-task masked factor study finds that isolated robot pixels and isolated
object pixels are each practically negligible even though whole coherent
futures steer strongly. The cross-model conclusion is therefore that coherent
future content matters, while its actionable carrier may be distributed,
context dependent, or architecture dependent.

The work does not show search over futures, expected-value comparison, stable
success/failure mediation, or general planning over task-object consequences.

## Claim-to-evidence map

| proposed claim | evidentiary status | permitted wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early LIBERO actions use reachable future endpoint content | confirmatory | Causal future-latent transplantation steers actions and executed endpoints toward natural donors across all ten tasks. |
| Cosmos 3 generalizes directional steering across tasks | confirmatory | Predicted donor steering is 0.992 [0.972, 1.010] and executed physical steering is 1.003 [0.950, 1.050] across 22 clusters and six tasks. |
| A future-token pathway carries Cosmos 3 steering | confirmatory | Token-count-preserving self-future K/V replacement removes 0.877 [0.842, 0.910] predicted-donor action projection, positive in every cluster. |
| The useful content is universally visible robot motion | rejected as a cross-model claim | Cosmos Policy evidence favors visible robot motion, but isolated robot pixels are practically negligible in the Cosmos 3 factor study. |
| Isolated object consequence content controls action | not meaningfully supported | Object-only effects are negligible under the registered 0.10 threshold in both content studies; this is not a universal absence claim. |
| Future use is uniform through the episode | rejected | Predict2 effects sharply attenuate after three and six open-loop chunks. |
| The future route is uniquely dominant | rejected | Equal-count current-key removal is more disruptive on average in Predict2. |
| Future mediation changes binary task success | not identified | No primary pair has a robust success/failure contrast across all registered continuation seeds. |

## Submission readiness

The empirical contribution is now substantially stronger than the previous
engineering study: it combines directional sufficiency, exact physical replay,
natural and Gaussian controls, a frozen cross-task population, token-preserving
pathway mediation, and a preregistered practical-equivalence factor test in a
current 16B model. The two frozen native-video failures are transparently
retained as missing rather than replaced; the prespecified population and
factor minima are both achieved.

Before submission, the remaining work is an independent artifact audit rather
than more outcome-driven experimentation: regenerate every table and figure
from the checked-in summaries in a clean environment, verify citations and
checkpoint licenses, add exact compute accounting, and have a reader challenge
the claim-to-evidence mapping. A larger cross-simulator replication would be
valuable future work but is not required to make the current central claim.
