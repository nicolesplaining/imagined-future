# Hybrid revision notes

## Editorial approach

This version uses the revised ZIP's conventional NeurIPS-facing organization and Nicole's newer scientific record. The six top-level sections are Introduction, Related Work, Methods, Results, Discussion and Limitations, and Conclusion. Inside Results, the paper retains the question-driven sequence:

1. Do coherent futures steer behavior?
2. What future content causes steering?
3. Which internal pathway carries the effect?

This shape follows the recurring pattern in the accepted NeurIPS workshop papers reviewed for the earlier revision: a short problem--gap--approach introduction, methods separated from findings, a small number of claim-led result sections, and one consolidated interpretation and limitations section.

## Scientific updates

- Replaced the four-state Cosmos 3 demonstration with the frozen 22-state, six-task population study.
- Added predicted- and executed-future steering, Gaussian and natural controls, task-balanced sensitivity analyses, sign counts, and transparent accounting for two incomplete states.
- Replaced the four-state calibration plus single Banana K/V story with the frozen population intervention: action and executed-endpoint suppression are positive in 22/22 states.
- Added the ten-state Cosmos 3 robot/object pixel-factor study and its prespecified practical-negligibility criterion.
- Restored the Cosmos Policy block-27 future-key intervention with its dose curve and controls, while avoiding a population-level claim because the available manuscript does not report its state count or confidence intervals.
- Preserved the Cosmos Policy directionality, modality, rendered-factor, natural-pair, later-state, and exploratory RoboCasa results.
- Removed the obsolete claim that Cosmos 3 has an unresolved endpoint inconsistency.

## Language and claim calibration

- Uses “one-chunk simulator endpoint” rather than language implying hardware execution.
- Scopes Cosmos Policy claims to the direct-policy mode; its separate best-of-N planning mode is not evaluated.
- Describes K/V results operationally as replacement loss and pathway evidence, not formal population mediation or a complete circuit decomposition.
- Treats the Cosmos Policy visible-robot-motion interpretation as model- and state-specific.
- States that the Cosmos 3 pixel study does not show joint necessity and may miss distributed or nonlinear content.
- Does not infer task success, candidate-future search, outcome comparison, or value-based planning.
